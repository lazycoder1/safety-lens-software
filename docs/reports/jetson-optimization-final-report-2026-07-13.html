01 · Executive verdict

Four unique live cameras are proven. Eight hybrid sources passed. Ten did not.

The optimization is real, but the labels around the number matter.

Use YOLO26 Small as the primary. Use RT-DETRv4-S only as a gated phone-recall specialist.

The model and scheduler optimizations remain valid: a short combined harness sustained 25 repeated RTSP connections and production-shaped inference. The later full-application ladder is the stronger deployment test: 6 and 8 total sources passed, while 10 failed after roughly four minutes with connection loss despite zero inference failures, overloads, or queue buildup.

Recommended now

Deployment claim today: 4 unique live RTSP cameras.

Hybrid application evidence: 8 total sources for 110 seconds; four were live RTSP and four were file-backed TMEIC clips.

Lab envelope: 25 repeated connections under a short harness. It is not a planning or sales cap.

Why the deployment claim stays at four. The 8-source pass mixed four live RTSP feeds with four looping files, so it did not add independent cameras, network paths, or NVR sessions. At 10 total sources the connection/recovery path failed under hybrid application pressure before the GPU saturated; the run does not isolate whether the first trigger was CPU scheduling, capture contention, or NVR behavior. The 25-session harness remains useful hardware evidence, but the full application has now shown a lower operational boundary.

03 · Final pipeline

Cheap decisions first; expensive models only when an alert remains possible.

This is the right conceptual pipeline for this Jetson.

1 · CaptureNVDEC, direct 640-wide output, 6 FPS cap, staggered connection startup.
2 · PrimaryYOLO26 Small INT8 at 4 effective FPS. Person, vehicle, animal, and coarse phone context.
3 · ContextFresh-result reuse, person geometry, tracking context, configured zones, schedules, and rule compilation.
4 · SpecialistPPE or RT-DETR only when needed. The specialist replaces a scheduled primary slot instead of becoming additive load.
5 · AlertAssociation, confidence, zone policy, persistence, delivery queue, retry, and health telemetry.

Why not a separate “COCO pre-filter”?

YOLO26 Small already is the COCO primary/filter. Adding another detector before it would duplicate decode, preprocessing, model execution, and postprocessing. The useful filter is the current primary result plus cheap geometry, track freshness, zones, schedules, and rule state.

Why substitution matters

Running PPE or RT-DETR on top of all four primary frames increases aggregate GPU demand. Replacing one due primary slot preserves a four-decision-per-second control loop while spending that slot on higher-value specialist evidence.

If forced to choose RT-DETR-S or YOLO Nano as the primary

Nano is computationally more plausible; RT-DETR-S is less than half the measured throughput of the current Small primary and has an engine-only freshness ceiling of 10 cameras at 4 FPS before PPE or transport. But there is no accuracy evidence here that Nano catches the required small/distant people, phones, vehicles, or PPE context. It should not be promoted on a speed assumption.

The actual choice

Neither replaces the current Small primary. Small has the measured accuracy/capacity contract. RT-DETR-S earns one device-wide frame per second only for phone-recall escalation. Nano remains a future experiment, not a conclusion.

Why Nano was not used. The stated product constraint was “Small, not Nano,” and the goal included acceptable accuracy, not camera count at any cost. Small-object recall is exactly where a Nano model is most likely to trade quality for speed. At the 25-camera boundary, capture/memory traffic and the FP16 PPE latency tail were material bottlenecks, so a faster primary alone would not automatically turn 25 into a safe production tier.
05 · How 4, 8, 10 and 25 were tested

The tests covered real decode and real inference, but not 25 unique full-stack cameras.

Here is the exact boundary between evidence and extrapolation.

Physical capture load

  1. Inventory found four reachable office NVR channels. Channels 5–16 were unavailable.
  2. The harness opened the four real main streams round-robin to reach 20–29 simultaneous RTSP sessions.
  3. Every connection used the real GStreamer/NVDEC path. Frame counts, minimum and aggregate FPS, open time, CPU time, and peak RSS were recorded.
  4. The final profile emitted 640×360, targeted 6 FPS, and staggered opens to avoid a connection storm.

Inference and specialist load

  1. The exact Jetson model server handled YOLO26 Small INT8 primary, YOLOE-26S FP16 PPE, and RT-DETRv4-S FP16.
  2. Every camera was scheduled for four effective decisions per second.
  3. The strict case forced one PPE FPS on every camera, not only the observed live duty, plus one RT-DETR FPS across the device.
  4. Requests went through the edge inference transport, admission limits, batch routes, and substitution scheduler.

A case was rejected even when every request completed if its worst latency crossed the 250 ms camera period. This is why 25 cameras at 960/8 and 640/8 were rejected, and why 640/6 became the selected profile.

Validation strength: two consecutive 60-second 25-camera inference runs used one stable set of 25 live decode sessions; both completed 6,000/6,000 effective decisions. Accuracy was separately replayed through the real phone and PPE worker/alert rules at 960 and 640 capture widths. Validation gap: a 60-second harness is a boundary benchmark, not an 8–24 hour production soak, and duplicated NVR sessions are not a substitute for 25 distinct feeds.

Full-application hybrid ladder

The existing four RTSP cameras stayed configured. Two, four, then six temporary file cameras replayed two 854×640, 8 FPS TMEIC H.264 clips through normal camera workers, primary/specialist execution plans, tracking, rules, stream publication, health, and persistence. Temporary alert thresholds were raised to avoid polluting production alerts.

25-session capture result

25/25 opened, minimum 5.942 FPS, 149.339 aggregate capture FPS, 72.33% of one CPU core, and 448.973 MB peak process RSS. Direct capture dimensions were 640×360.

Accuracy preservation at direct 640 capture

RT-DETR phone outcomes stayed 5/6 positives and 0/4 negatives with identical actionable per-image outcomes. All 18 PPE frames and all 18 PPE violation-rule outcomes matched the prior 960 capture path.

Post-test recovery is part of the result: all six temporary cameras and their copied media were removed, leaving exactly the original four configurations. Cameras 1, 3 and 4 recovered promptly; camera 2 ignored ordinary restarts and a 30-second cooldown, then recovered fresh on NVDEC only after its worker was paused for 90 seconds. All four finished fresh on NVDEC and inference/alert queues were empty. Requiring manual extended cooldown still makes the 10-source tier an operational failure.
07 · Experiment ledger

What was tried, promoted, and rejected.

Rejected experiments are part of the result: they prevented attractive averages from becoming bad production changes.

Primary model and resolution
  • YOLO26 Small INT8 640: promoted as the only universal COCO primary.
  • 512 primary: rejected; lost person context needed for downstream gates.
  • 608 primary: rejected; fixed-engine routing did not create a safe gain.
  • 960/832 phone probes: 832 was faster but did not improve actionable alert outcomes; periodic high-resolution YOLO probing was retired.
  • Person crop: rejected; doubled work, did not improve phone recall, and created a negative.
  • YOLO Nano: intentionally not used or benchmarked; no parity evidence.
RT-DETR and phone recall
  • RT-DETR Large: rejected after Jetson unified-memory failures.
  • RT-DETRv4-S: exported, parity checked, built as FP16 batch-1/batch-2, and benchmarked; rejected as primary, promoted conceptually as one-FPS device-wide gated specialist.
  • RF-DETR Small: built at opset 16 but rejected for latency, false positive, memory, and integration cost.
  • RT substitution: selected over additive RT work; keeps four effective decisions per camera.
  • Phone association geometry: widened horizontally to recover handheld-phone placement without enabling raw RT false positives.
  • One-second context hold: rejected; increased specialist duty without recovering an extra helmet event.
Batching and scheduling
  • Edge-side microbatch: promoted; batching before HTTP avoids model-server queue ambiguity.
  • Batch-2 and batch-4: promoted; batch-4 raised isolated primary throughput to 104.643 FPS.
  • Batch-8: rejected; throughput/tail behavior did not beat batch-4 safely.
  • Adaptive 2/4 routing: promoted with a 6 ms early flush for exact pairs.
  • 14 ms partial-group rendezvous: promoted; reduced expensive singleton remainder work.
  • Direct singleton bypass: rejected; worse p95 and maximum latency.
  • Model-server cross-camera batching: rejected; timeout, ownership, and tail behavior were worse.
  • Work-weighted camera phase spacing: rejected; shifted bursts without a reliable boundary gain.
  • 0.70 partial-phase weight: promoted for the final remainder group.
  • 3 FPS primary: rejected; reduced cadence without producing a defensible capacity gain.
Specialist control
  • Rule/context gating: promoted; specialists run only when their rule can become actionable.
  • Tracked PPE substitution: promoted with 0.5 FPS scout and 1 FPS confirmation cadence.
  • Primary/PPE overlap: concurrent independent execution retained; forced serialization increased queue occupancy.
  • Specialist microbatch: promoted for compatible primary/PPE pairs.
  • PPE INT8: rejected despite 24% mean speed gain; detections collapsed from 13 to 4 on the 18-image corpus.
  • YOLOE boxes-only postprocess: rejected; did not improve the supported boundary.
  • ByteTrack as a boolean filter: rejected; added tail and sparse IDs at low FPS. Tracking remains useful when dwell/identity features require it.
Frames and transport
  • Immutable captured inference frame: promoted; stopped an asynchronous path from copying/mutating the wrong frame.
  • Raw local BGR transport: promoted; avoided local JPEG encode/decode.
  • Byte-cast memoryview: promoted; avoided a full 960×540 BGR copy, measured about 249× cheaper than tobytes().
  • Bounded admission: retained; excess work drops visibly instead of building an unbounded stale queue.
  • Metadata caching/breaker: retained to avoid repeated control-plane model metadata calls during failure.
Capture and deployment
  • NVDEC output dropping: used to align 25 FPS source streams to the useful capture rate.
  • Direct 640 NVDEC/VIC output: promoted after phone/PPE alert parity replay.
  • Uniform live 640 output: FRONT/cam2 was moved from its 352×288 NVR substream to channel 2 main. A 30-sample post-restart soak kept all four feeds fresh on NVDEC with zero connection failures, inference failures, or overload drops; FRONT sustained about 2.74 effective AI FPS.
  • 6 FPS capture cap: promoted; 8 FPS crossed the PPE tail at 25, while 6 preserved 4 FPS AI plus live-view headroom.
  • 100 ms startup stagger: promoted; concurrent 29-session open was unreliable, while 50–250 ms stagger opened all 29.
  • Docker NVIDIA runtime preservation: repaired after an earlier recreation silently fell back to CPU decode.
  • Physical combined boundary: 25 pass, 26 provisional, 27 reject.
  • UI/API port mapping: restored in final cleanup after a portless swap broke browser connectivity.

09 · “Connecting to server…”

The UI was correct: its API endpoint was unreachable from the browser.

This was not GPU overload and not evidence that the 25-session model test had failed.

Root cause

The login page polls http://<Jetson host>:8000/api/ping every three seconds. The frontend was reachable on port 3030, but the edge container had no host binding for port 8000. Host and remote-browser curls both returned connection refused. The frontend Nginx configuration served static files only and had no API proxy; the frontend and edge containers were also on different Docker networks.

Why it coexisted with “25-camera support”

The load harness called the model server/edge path inside the Jetson’s Docker network, where inference was healthy. The UI runs in a user’s browser and needs the host-published port. Compute-plane success and browser control-plane reachability are separate contracts; only the first had been checked after the portless swaps.

Timeline and cleanup

  • All preserved production edge containers before 12 July 2026 22:52 exposed 8000:8000.
  • The first portless production replacement appeared at that time; later swaps cloned its empty port set.
  • On 13 July, the current hash-identical image was recreated with all inspected environment, mounts, devices, NVIDIA runtime, restart policy, and network preserved; only 8000:8000 was restored.
  • The temporary volumes-from dependency was removed by materializing the same ten mounts directly. The prior container remains as rollback.
  • Acceptance: external /api/ping HTTP 200, Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, both cameras fresh, and both on gstreamer_nvdec.
Outcome: the login status now has the network contract it needs to show “Server ready.” If a browser still displays the old text, reload the page once to clear an already-open failed polling cycle; the server endpoint itself is now reachable.
Browser verification on this evidence cut: Browser Use reached http://<Jetson host>:3030/login and rendered “Server ready.” The saved/default admin password was no longer valid, so the authenticated camera-grid test could not be completed without an operator sign-in. Source review confirms 2×2 and 3×3 modes: four or nine visible tiles per page, with pagination for overflow.
10 · Close-out

This chapter is complete. Stop tuning and validate the deployment claim.

The remaining work is evidence collection, not another model or scheduler tweak.

Final architecture: YOLO26 Small primary + context/zone rules + conditional PPE and RT-DETR specialists.

Keep 4 effective AI FPS per camera, 6 FPS capture, direct 640 NVDEC, batch-4 primary/PPE, a 0.70 remainder phase, and one aggregate RT-DETR frame per second. Do not switch the primary to RT-DETR. Do not switch to Nano without a site-labelled accuracy and alert-parity gate.

Sell/plan today: 4 unique live cameras on this Jetson/site combination.

Provisional application evidence: 8 hybrid sources for 110 seconds. Do not convert that into eight live cameras.

Next gate: 6 then 8 distinct RTSP cameras, at least two hours each, full application workers, real zones/rules, alert persistence/delivery, reconnects, UI rendering, and thermal telemetry.

Do not claim 20 or 25 production cameras. Claim “4 unique live cameras proven; 8 hybrid sources passed briefly; 10 hybrid sources failed late; 25 is a short repeated-session hardware envelope.” That statement matches every retained result.
